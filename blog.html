How to Prepare for an Accounting Interview: A Practical Checklist

A focused preparation guide for accounting candidates applying for AP, AR, reconciliation, R2R and general ledger roles.

By DigiCareer Editorial Team · Updated 25 November 2026 · 5 min read

An accounting interview usually tests more than definitions. Employers want to know whether you can explain a process, work with supporting documents, investigate differences and communicate an accurate result.

1. Understand the role before the interview

Read the job description and identify the work involved. Accounts Payable may focus on invoice processing and vendor balances. Accounts Receivable may involve customer collections and receipts. R2R and General Ledger roles may include journals, reconciliations, close activities and reporting.

2. Prepare examples from your own work

Prepare two or three examples using this structure: the issue, the checks you performed, the action you took and the final outcome. Use real examples and do not disclose confidential company or customer information.

3. Revise core accounting concepts

  • Debit and credit rules and journal entries.
  • Bank, vendor and customer reconciliation.
  • Accruals, prepayments and provisions.
  • Trial balance and general ledger review.
  • Month-end close and supporting schedules.
  • Basic GST and TDS concepts relevant to the role.

4. Practise Excel with accounting data

Be ready to explain how you use filters, sorting, PivotTables, XLOOKUP or VLOOKUP, SUMIFS, COUNTIF and conditional formatting. Practise matching two transaction lists and explaining how you investigate unmatched items.

5. Explain your reconciliation method clearly

A strong answer describes the source data, matching fields, amount and date checks, duplicate review, exception ageing, supporting evidence and final sign-off. Explain what you do when a transaction has no approval or supporting document.

6. Prepare questions for the interviewer

  • Which accounting process and systems does the team use?
  • What are the daily volumes and key performance measures?
  • What does the month-end responsibility look like?
  • How is training provided for the process?

Final checklist

Keep your resume consistent with your actual experience. Review the company and job description, test your audio or travel route, keep examples ready and be honest about tools you have not used. A clear explanation of your real work is stronger than an exaggerated claim.
