How to Prepare for an R2R Interview: Topics, Scenarios and Sample Answers

26 November 2026 · 7 min read

A Record to Report (R2R) interview usually tests more than accounting definitions. Interviewers want to know whether you understand how transactions move into the general ledger, how balances are validated and how a period is closed with a clear audit trail.

1. Understand the R2R process from start to finish

Be ready to explain the flow in plain language: transactions are recorded in sub-ledgers or source systems, reviewed and posted to the general ledger, reconciled, adjusted where supported, and included in period-end reporting. The exact division of work differs by organisation, so describe the process you have actually handled rather than assuming every company follows the same structure.

2. Revise the core accounting topics

  • General ledger: account structure, debit and credit balances, control accounts and trial balance review.
  • Journal entries: accruals, prepayments, reclassifications, corrections, reversals and supporting approvals.
  • Balance-sheet reconciliations: comparing the ledger with independent support, identifying breaks and documenting ageing items.
  • Month-end close: cut-off checks, recurring journals, reconciliations, review sign-off and close trackers.
  • Intercompany: matching reciprocal balances and investigating timing, currency or posting differences where applicable.
  • Variance analysis: comparing actual balances with prior periods or expectations and explaining material movements.

3. Prepare one real example for each major skill

Choose examples you can explain without sharing confidential company information. For a reconciliation example, describe the source records, how you matched references and amounts, what exceptions you found, how you followed up and how closure was evidenced. For a journal example, explain the reason, calculation, support and approval route.

Use the STAR structure to keep answers organised: Situation (what needed attention), Task (your responsibility), Action (the checks and steps you performed) and Result (the verified outcome). Do not invent percentages, transaction volumes or savings. If you do not know the exact figure, explain the scale honestly.

4. Practise common scenario questions

“The ledger balance does not agree with the supporting schedule. What would you do?”

Confirm the period, account and source version first. Reconcile line by line using references, dates and amounts. Classify timing items, omissions, duplicates and posting errors. Obtain missing support, document the owner and next action, and propose an adjustment only when evidence and approval requirements are met.

“A journal is requested just before the close deadline. How do you handle it?”

Understand the business reason and accounting period. Check the calculation, account coding, supporting documents and required approval. Consider whether the entry is complete and accurate, record the review trail, and escalate if evidence is missing rather than posting an unsupported journal to meet a deadline.

“How do you prioritise multiple open reconciliations?”

Use risk, value, ageing, close dependency and due date. Work first on material or deadline-critical items, communicate blockers early and keep a tracker showing status, owner and next action.

5. Explain your Excel skills with a practical example

Do not only list functions. Explain how you use them. For example, XLOOKUP can retrieve a matching reference, SUMIFS can compare totals across a defined period or category, COUNTIFS can identify repeated combinations, and PivotTables can summarise open items by reason or owner. Mention the checks you perform to catch blanks, duplicate keys, date-format problems and false matches.

6. Prepare questions for the interviewer

  • Which activities sit within this team's R2R scope?
  • Which ERP and reconciliation tools are used?
  • How are account reconciliations reviewed and signed off?
  • What are the main close deadlines and hand-offs?
  • How is success measured during the first three months?
Before the interview

Prepare a 60-second explanation of your current responsibilities, two detailed process examples, one exception-handling scenario and a truthful list of the systems and Excel tools you have used. Be clear about what you know and what you are ready to learn.
