How to Verify a Job Posting in India Before You Apply

Published 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

A job post can look convincing and still leave important questions unanswered. Before sending your resume or personal details, take a few minutes to confirm who is hiring, what the role involves and how the application process works.

This checklist is designed for job seekers in India. It does not guarantee that an opportunity is genuine, but it gives you a repeatable way to identify warning signs and verify information through independent channels.

1. Confirm the employer's identity

Look for the employer's full name, official website, business address and a clear description of its work. Search for the vacancy on the company's own careers page. If the post names a staffing agency, identify both the agency and the end client where that information is disclosed.

Be careful with names that closely imitate a known company. Check the website address character by character instead of relying on a logo or display name.

2. Check how the recruiter can be contacted

Compare the recruiter's email domain with the employer's official website. A free email address does not automatically prove fraud, and a company-style address does not automatically prove authenticity. If you are unsure, contact the organisation through the phone number or careers contact published on its official website and ask whether the recruiter and vacancy are authorised.

Review the recruiter's work history and public professional profile for consistency, but do not treat a social profile as the only verification.

3. Read the job description carefully

A genuine job description should give enough information for you to decide whether the work fits your experience. Check for:

  • Job title, team and main responsibilities
  • Required skills, tools and experience level
  • Work location, shift, travel and work-mode expectations
  • Employment type and reporting structure
  • Salary range or a clear explanation of when compensation will be discussed
  • Application deadline and a traceable application route

Compare the requirements with your actual skills. If the role is described only as “easy work, high salary” with no duties or employer details, ask for a proper job description before proceeding.

4. Never pay to secure an interview or job

Be cautious if someone asks you to pay an application fee, interview fee, refundable security deposit, document-verification charge or payment for a guaranteed offer. The Government of India's National Career Service (NCS) states that its registration, job application and interview-related services are free and warns job seekers not to pay fees to employers.

If a message claims to be from NCS, verify it on the official NCS website. Do not use a payment link or contact number supplied only in an unverified message.

5. Understand the interview process

Ask how many stages are involved, who will conduct them and whether any assessment is required. A technical test or work sample may be reasonable when it relates to the role, but requests for extensive unpaid work, account access or unusual software installation deserve scrutiny.

Use a video or phone interview arranged through a verifiable company channel. Do not install unknown remote-access applications or share screen access to your banking or payment apps.

6. Protect your personal information

A resume normally needs enough contact and work information for a recruiter to assess your application. Do not send OTPs, passwords, card details or internet-banking credentials. Be cautious about sharing Aadhaar, PAN, bank account details or identity documents before you have verified the employer and reached a legitimate stage of onboarding.

If documents are genuinely required later, ask why they are needed, how they will be collected and how they will be protected. Use the employer's verified process rather than a random file-sharing link.

7. Check the offer before accepting

Read the offer letter for the legal employer name, designation, work location, compensation structure, employment type, joining date, notice terms and conditions of employment. Confirm that the sender's email domain and contact details match the organisation you verified earlier.

If the offer is unusually urgent, contains unexplained deductions or differs from what was discussed, pause and request written clarification from an independently verified contact.

A simple apply-or-pause decision

  • Apply: the employer is identifiable, the role is clear, the application route is credible and the process does not demand suspicious payments or credentials.
  • Ask for clarification: the employer appears real but the recruiter, client, salary, location or process is unclear.
  • Pause and report: the contact asks for money, OTPs, banking access or sensitive documents without a verified reason, or impersonates a known organisation.
DigiCareer reminder

A good opportunity should withstand basic questions. Verify the employer independently, understand the work, protect your personal information and keep a copy of the job description and correspondence.

Official reference: National Career Service, Ministry of Labour & Employment, Government of India. NCS information applies to NCS services; always verify the terms and identity of other job platforms and employers separately.
